Call or text 988, or chat at 988lifeline.org. This page is a public brief on one bridge. It is not a crisis service, and it does not describe how anyone died.

The span · the Piscataqua River Bridge

A barrier was recommended. No decision has been published.

A 2026 engineering study recommended a barrier for the Interstate 95 span between Portsmouth and Kittery. The two states that own the bridge had not published a decision as of 12 October 2026. This page sets out what the public reporting says, what is still unknown, and how strong the evidence on barriers is. It does not ask you to do anything.

Where things stand

Two systems recommended. The decision belongs to two departments.

The engineering firm Stantec recommended two barrier systems, a wire net or a rod fence, for the full length of the Interstate 95 span. The decision belongs to the Maine and New Hampshire departments of transportation. As of 12 October 2026 they had not published which system goes forward, or when.

Which bridge

The high-level Interstate 95 span, the Piscataqua River Bridge, owned jointly by the Maine and New Hampshire departments of transportation. Not the Memorial Bridge. Not the Sarah Mildred Long Bridge. A harbormaster does not own it, and the Coast Guard does not own it.

The count

There is no published annual count of deaths from this span. In December 2025 the Portsmouth Herald counted three deaths from this span and the Sarah Mildred Long Bridge in a two-week period that spring. In April 2026 it wrote that the cluster included two from the high-level bridge. Read those as one cluster, not two totals. The same reporting says crises here go back years. Any figure taken to the departments needs a source: call-outs counted as call-outs, deaths as a documented list. Without one, the accurate summary is: no census, a known span, a barrier unbuilt.

Where the file sits

Stantec’s mid-March 2026 report recommended a wire net or a rod fence. In April the press wrote that no decision had been made. Stevens has described more than twelve thousand hours of engineering still required, and a cost near three to five million dollars. Cass, in December 2025, put a definitive proposal no earlier than summer 2026. Stevens described about six months of structural analysis after the study. The study landed in March. On 12 October 2026 a search of the public reporting found no chosen system, no end date, and no budget year. If a decision was published after that check, this page is out of date.

What the departments have said

The two departments of transportation own the bridge and the decision. In May 2025 the New Hampshire DOT could already point at a new concrete rail, from a preservation project of about 60 million dollars, and say it was harder to climb. Lane also said a fence could hazard traffic, because the shoulder opens as a travel lane. A later study recommended a barrier, and named the shoulder problem as something the next phase has to design for. The open question is how the next design phase answers the shoulder and wind constraints.

Six options

The options that have been discussed.

One has a study behind it. Two are already in place or already the job. Three are not a barrier.

01 · Recommended in April 2026. Not decided.

A barrier the full length of the span

This is the option with the study behind it. The Portsmouth Herald reported that Stantec kept two systems, a wire net and a rod fence, from a set of vertical designs, because they deter access and still allow emergency crews and inspection gear. WMUR, the next day, said the study reviewed eight systems. The report is not posted; the disagreement stays. Stantec cited a Swiss finding that a full-height barrier, ten to eleven feet, cut deaths by more than 80 percent at the sites studied. That is not a promise for this bridge, and no height for this design has been published. A 2020 Cochrane review (Okolie and colleagues) also found fewer deaths at sites after barriers and nets, graded the evidence low-quality, and said it is still unknown whether deaths move to other sites. That last gap is why the other two bridges stay on the page.

02 · Announced May 2025. Up by the April 2026 reporting.

Signs for 988

Crisis-line signs went on the high-level bridge and on the Sarah Mildred Long Bridge. Signs are in place. Stantec wrote that signs, phones and lighting can help and do not restrain anyone.

03 · Already the job. Not a barrier.

Cameras, patrols, a phone, a talk-down

Officers and a harbormaster save particular people on particular days. They are called after someone is already on the span. On the Penobscot Narrows Bridge, the Bangor Daily News reported in March 2025 that hotline phones had been in place about ten years and were out of order at the time of at least two deaths. Patrols and phones respond once someone is on the span. They are not a barrier.

04 · New Hampshire’s answer in May 2025. The study still asked for more.

Call the rebuilt rail enough

Jennifer Lane said the concrete rail, from a preservation project of about 60 million dollars, was harder to climb than the old one, and that screening had been added on structural members. She also said a fence could hazard traffic, because the shoulder opens as a travel lane in heavy flow. The later feasibility study treated the shoulder as a design constraint for the next phase. The rebuilt rail is not the system that study recommended.

05 · Still the live option. The dates they named have passed.

Wait for the engineering

Phase two is wind, ice, and whether a barrier shortens the life of the bridge. Cass named the life of the bridge as the concern. At the Penobscot Narrows Bridge, wind testing added about a year to the fence schedule. Cass said a definitive proposal no earlier than summer 2026. Stevens, in December 2025, described about six months of structural analysis after the study. The study landed in March. On 12 October 2026 no published decision was found.

06 · Not solved by a fence on this one.

The other two bridges

People have also died from the Sarah Mildred Long Bridge, and the Memorial Bridge is the third span between Portsmouth and Kittery. A barrier on the interstate does not cover them. The Cochrane review says substitution to other sites is not settled. Saying so keeps the brief honest. The high-level bridge is the only one of the three with a completed barrier study.

The precedent in Maine

Penobscot Narrows: nineteen years, then one quiet year.

The Penobscot Narrows Bridge is the closest comparison: a similar delay, in the same state. The Bangor Daily News reported that the fence took nineteen years and twelve deaths. That is a total across nineteen years, not an annual rate. The fence went up in May 2025, after a 2023 bill of about 2 million dollars, 1.2 million of it state money plus a federal share. In May 2026 the same paper reported no known deaths from that span in the following year, and fewer crisis calls: one from the sheriff’s office and one from the State Police. One year is not a lifetime proof. It is the closest finished case on this coast. The paper also reported, in March 2025, a 2005 study of Augusta’s Memorial Bridge: none recorded after a fence went up in 1983, against 14 in the 23 years before. One study. One bridge. Not this one.
