The stations · who already does this work

An inventory, not a ranking.

Wood Island is first because it is the building. It is not on watch. The boat that answers this harbor is Station Portsmouth Harbor, in New Castle. The rest are the services a pilot from this lab might be written for: some volunteer societies two centuries old, some state agencies on coasts where the toll is still rising. Founding years and descriptions are as each service gives them.

The inventory

1908–1948 · Kittery, Maine · U.S. Life-Saving Service, then Coast Guard

Wood Island Life-Saving Station

A Duluth-type station, George R. Tolman’s design, built by Sugden Brothers of Portsmouth to replace Jerry’s Point across the harbor. Maine Preservation, writing in 2026, credits the surfmen with more than 60 major rescues and hundreds of lives saved across the station’s four decades. Down East, in 2022, reported a tighter figure from records the station’s nonprofit unearthed: 1,678 seafarers aided between 1918 and 1939. Aided is not saved, and that count is not the full forty years. The Navy took the watch in the Second World War; the station was decommissioned in 1948. The living rescue watch for this harbor is Station Portsmouth Harbor. Wood Island is the classroom, not the on-duty boat. The history, and the disagreements.

1824 · United Kingdom and Ireland · volunteer lifeboats, charity

Royal National Lifeboat Institution

Founded after William Hillary’s appeal. The crews are mostly volunteers. The 2025 annual report counts 147,096 lives saved since 1824, and defines a life saved as someone who would have died had the RNLI not been there: a judgment, written down the same way each year, which is why it can be used. In 2025 the lifeboats saved 272 people and the lifeguards 117. What travels is the method: a station, a boat that can launch, a log. The row.

1915 · United States · federal search and rescue

United States Coast Guard

Heir of the Revenue Cutter Service, from 1790, and the Life-Saving Service, formalised in 1878; the two were joined in 1915. Surfmen with a beach apparatus became a national system of boats, cutters and aircraft. Station Portsmouth Harbor is the unit a call from this island still reaches. The service’s own count for 2024.

1824 · The Netherlands · volunteer lifeboats

KNRM

The Koninklijke Nederlandse Redding Maatschappij carries the Dutch volunteer tradition that began the same year as Hillary’s appeal. The North Sea is a short, steep, cold problem. Their relevance to this lab is the small-boat rescue in bad water, not the size of the fleet.

1865 · Germany · volunteer sea rescue, independent

DGzRS

The Seenotretter, independent of the state and tasked beside it. A life-saving service does not have to be a navy to be the crew that arrives. Independence has a cost, which is funding, and a benefit, which is a single purpose.

1967 · France · volunteer rescue at sea

SNSM

The Société Nationale de Sauvetage en Mer brought together societies whose roots run to the 1860s. France also has a state maritime authority. The split between the volunteer boat and the state coordination is the operating picture on much of the European coast.

1891 · Norway · volunteer rescue society

Redningsselskapet

Built for a coast of weather and fishing: a long coast, cold water, and vessels that work in it on purpose. The cold-water clock that the June lab teaches is their ordinary day.

1907 · Sweden · volunteer sea rescue

Sjöräddningssällskapet

A Baltic and west-coast volunteer fleet. The Baltic is also the water of the Gustloff and the Estonia; the same chart holds a volunteer station and a wartime toll, and both belong in the inventory.

1962 · Canada · federal rescue coordination

Canadian Coast Guard

Search and rescue on three oceans and the Great Lakes, coordinating ships, aircraft and volunteers. Relevant here because the Gulf of Maine is a shared water with the United States: a drift that starts off Kittery can become someone else’s search.

1967 · South Africa · volunteer rescue

NSRI

The National Sea Rescue Institute. Crews are volunteers. A southern-hemisphere service with the same structure as the older European societies: stations, volunteers, a log.

1990 · Australia · federal coordination, state volunteers

AMSA and volunteer marine rescue

The Australian Maritime Safety Authority coordinates; volunteer marine rescue units do much of the near-shore work. Distance is the Australian version of the clock: the asset is far, so the signal and the raft matter more than heroics.

Volunteer federation · New Zealand · volunteer rescue units

Coastguard New Zealand

Units around both islands, tasked with the national coordination centre. Included because the adopter of a student pilot may be a unit this size, not a ministry.

1948 · Japan · federal maritime safety

Japan Coast Guard

Formed as the Maritime Safety Agency after the war. A full state service: patrol, search and rescue, and the hard problem of a coast with earthquakes, tsunamis and a large fishing fleet. The tsunami is the case where the life-saving station is itself the casualty.

1978 · India · federal maritime force

Indian Coast Guard

Search and rescue on a huge coastline with a vast small-boat fishery. The small-boat majority is the point: treaties written for ships of 24 metres do not reach the boats that die most often, and a pilot that only works for a flagged merchant ship has aimed at the counted rather than the killed.

1967 · Philippines · federal maritime safety and rescue

Philippine Coast Guard

Works the archipelago that produced the Doña Paz and the Princess of the Stars. Ferry disasters here are a design condition, not rare-event theory: many islands, many passengers, manifests that do not match the people aboard. A register that trusts the ticket count will undercount the dead.

1972 · Indonesia · national search and rescue

BASARNAS

A national search-and-rescue agency across an archipelago. Search, not only sea rescue: aviation and disaster sit in the same agency. Included so the inventory does not pretend life-saving at sea is only a European lifeboat house.

State maritime authority · Greece · coordination on a front-line migration sea

Hellenic Coast Guard

The state service on the water where the IOM toll is still rising. People are dying now, in boats, in one sea, and a coast guard is the institution on the radio. The row.

State services · China · state maritime law and rescue

China Coast Guard and maritime SAR

A large state system; public casualty series are thin. Listed because omitting the largest fishing nation would misrepresent the inventory. Public, comparable death figures are poor, and that gap belongs in the register team’s work, not in a footnote that flatters the countries which publish.
