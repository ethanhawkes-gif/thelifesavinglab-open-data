# Funder one-pager

*Figures are planning estimates, not quotes. Everything is proposed. Written 15 Oct 2026.*

## What the money would do

Put 24 students, six teams, on Portsmouth Harbor for two days in June 2027 (proposed 5–6 Jun) with the people who run rescues there, to size the drowning problem from open data, write one-page pilots, and test them in a staged rescue with a licensed crew. Co-designed with Applied Wisdom for Nonprofits, whose eight insights are the syllabus.

No quotes have been obtained; quotes are due by 18 Dec 2026. The biggest swings are whether a donor provides the drones and surface robots, and whether lodging and on-water support come in kind. Program design and leadership are contributed in kind by Ethan Hawkes. Applied Wisdom is not being asked for funding. Whether Signalry contributes seed funding is a decision not yet made.

## Where the money goes

Donations go directly to the Wood Island Life Saving Station Association, which restored the 1908 station and keeps it standing. The lab holds no funds and runs no account.

## What you would get, and what you would not

You would get numbers anyone can check, starting at zero: pilots written, pilots taken up by a named adopter, pilots still alive six months on, and pilots that failed and were published. You would get the open ledger, the tree, the register and the watch, all free to reuse. You would not get a count of lives saved: nobody counted them, and the lab does not report what nobody counted.

## How the lab makes its figures

Every figure on the site sits in a ledger row with its source, its year, its scope, a grade for its provenance (A primary official, B peer-reviewed, C press or secondary, D a judgment stated as such) and a rule for what it must not be added to. The global toll is given as a range, a floor of about 260,000 a year and a ceiling of about 415,000, and the unknown is drawn as unknown.
