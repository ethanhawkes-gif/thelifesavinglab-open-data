# Partner one-pager · harbors, stations, schools, outfitters

*For anyone who works this water or teaches near it. Everything here is proposed; no organisation named below has been contacted unless it says so. Written 15 Oct 2026.*

## In one paragraph

The Life-Saving Lab is a teaching and research project on Portsmouth Harbor, co-designed with Applied Wisdom for Nonprofits, that puts students and practitioners on the same problem: how many people drown, where the counting fails, and what one change this season could do about it. It publishes everything it makes, in the open, with sources. It dispatches no one and replaces nothing. In distress on the water: VHF channel 16 or the Coast Guard.

## What we ask of a partner (pick one)

| Partner | One thing we would ask | What comes back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A harbormaster's office | Run the incident register for one season: one row per event, fatal or not, two minutes each. No names. | The first published "minutes from alarm to reach" for this harbor, and a say in which pilots run. |
| An outfitter or paddling club | Try one pilot: the late-boat watch or the cold-water card. | A forecast written in advance, the result printed beside it, and the credit. |
| The Wood Island Life Saving Station Association | The site, the station's story, access for two days in June (proposed 5–6 Jun 2027). | An education program at the station; visitors and volunteers; a future that matches the station's past. |
| Coast Guard Station Portsmouth Harbor | A safety review of the staged rescue; a crew for the lift on day two. (Not contacted.) | Pilots aimed at the station's actual problems; nothing that competes with the station. |
| A university (UNH, Shoals Marine Lab, Cornell, MIT) | A faculty sponsor; student reach; possibly credit. (Not contacted.) | An experiential leadership lab with a real problem and an open record. |
| A robotics donor | Drones or a surface robot with operators, for two days. (To be identified.) | A public test of rescue robotics against a mark in chop, results published either way. |

## The rules we keep

No names, contacts or precise positions in any data. No figure summed across sources or waters. Failures published beside forecasts. A person reads every change before it is published. Pilots are judged by whether a practitioner would keep running them, and counted again six months on.

## What exists today

A sized global toll (a floor of about 260,000 drownings a year and a ceiling of about 416,000, with the unknown drawn as unknown); a ledger of 61 sourced figures; a decision tree of 17 branches, each with graded measures; an incident register you can use on a phone at the ramp; an hourly watch of reported drownings; a board of tasks anyone's AI assistant can take.

## Money

The lab holds none. Donations go directly to the Wood Island Life Saving Station Association. Boats, drones, classroom days and seasons of rows are gifts in kind and are credited by name of organisation, never by name of person in the data.
